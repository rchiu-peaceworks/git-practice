# Market Scan

Research question: how do English- and Chinese-speaking visitors currently find high-craftsmanship Japanese restaurants, and what do the existing sources miss?

| # | Source | What it covers | How places are chosen | Language | What it misses about craftsmanship |
|---|--------|----------------|------------------------|----------|--------------------------------------|
| 1 | **Tabelog** | Japan's largest restaurant review/ranking site — 800,000+ listings, 50M+ reviews | Algorithmic score weighted by reviewer credibility; strict scale (3.5+ is genuinely excellent, 4.0+ is Michelin-tier) | Japanese-first; English site exists and now supports reservations, but far shallower than the JP version | Score reflects overall satisfaction (taste, service, value) for a *local* palate — it doesn't surface *why* a place is a craft restaurant (lineage, years of single-discipline focus, technique). A 3.6 soba shop run by a third-generation master and a 3.6 solid neighborhood spot look identical in the score. |
| 2 | **Tripadvisor** | Tourist-oriented reviews/rankings worldwide | User reviews, mostly from foreign visitors | Full English/multilingual | Reviews are dominated by tourists rating tourist experiences (service in English, photogenic plating, convenience) — locals barely use it in Japan, so it structurally can't tell craft from crowd-pleasing. |
| 3 | **Google Maps** | Search + reviews tied to location/navigation | Aggregate star rating + review volume/foot traffic | Full multilingual | Ratings correlate with tourist foot traffic near stations/attractions, not food quality — a 4.7-4.9 near Asakusa or Shibuya is a strong tourist-trap signal, not a craft signal. No concept of "single-discipline" or "generational" at all. |
| 4 | **Pocket Concierge** | English-language reservation platform, 800+ partner restaurants including hidden gems recommended by other chefs | Curated partner list; mixes famous/media-featured spots with lesser-known ones vetted by chef recommendation | Full English, no-hassle booking | Surfaces some genuinely great craft restaurants, but "recommended by chefs" isn't disclosed per-listing as a filter — a visitor can't screen specifically for single-discipline/generational lineage without already knowing what to look for. |
| 5 | **Michelin Guide (Japan)** | Star ratings for fine dining, judged on product quality, technique mastery, chef's personality, consistency | Anonymous professional inspectors | Full English site | Closest existing source to a "craftsmanship" signal, but it's fine-dining/star-chasing by design — it excludes humble, inexpensive, single-focus shops (e.g. a small unagi counter with no ambition beyond unagi) that fit our niche but will never chase a star. |
| 6 | **byFood** | English reservation + guide platform, ~13,500 restaurants in Japan (2,528+ in Tokyo alone), human-written listings | Restaurants personally contacted and onboarded; broad coverage from izakaya to kaiseki | Full English, human (non-auto-translate) descriptions | Optimized for *access* (no-Japanese-needed booking), not for craft discovery — breadth over depth, no lineage/technique filter. |
| 7 | **Gurunavi** | Restaurant listing + real-time booking, ~50,000 restaurants | Pay-to-list model — restaurants supply their own listing info | English, Chinese, Korean, Japanese | Since restaurants pay to be featured, the platform favors businesses with marketing budgets, not necessarily the quiet, single-focus craft shops that don't advertise. |
| 8 | **Savor Japan** | English/Chinese/Korean gourmet guide, ~2,000 restaurants, backed by Japan Tourism Bureau | Curated selection with actual chef bios/philosophy included | English, Traditional & Simplified Chinese, Korean | Closest model to what we want (chef stories, philosophy) — but curation criteria aren't disclosed as "craft-first," and coverage (2,000 restaurants) still leans toward established/fine-dining names over undiscovered generational shops. |
| 9 | **Time Out Tokyo** | Editorial "best of" lists — new openings, classics, Michelin spots, cheap eats | Editorial team + local expert long-list, refined by editor judgment | Full English (global Time Out network) | Editorial judgment optimizes for "best meal for any visitor" (variety, buzz, value) — not for a strict craft/lineage test, so trendy and traditional get lumped together with no way to filter for the latter. |
| 10 | **TikTok / Instagram viral food content** | Short-form video recommendations, often driving sudden foot traffic to specific spots | Algorithmic virality + influencer choice, no editorial standard at all | Whatever language the creator posts in, rarely localized | The clearest driver of the exact problem we're solving — virality rewards visual spectacle and novelty, actively pulls tourists toward the *same* handful of spots regardless of craft, and can overwhelm small traditional vendors who never asked to go viral (see the 2026 "Tokyo pudding" controversy). |

## The gap

Every existing source optimizes for one of: **overall satisfaction** (Tabelog, Google Maps), **tourist accessibility** (byFood, Gurunavi, Pocket Concierge), **fine-dining prestige** (Michelin), **editorial buzz** (Time Out), or **virality** (TikTok/Instagram) — none of them filter for *craftsmanship itself*: a single discipline, practiced under one chef or lineage for years, independent of price point, fame, or photogenicity. Savor Japan gets closest (chef bios, philosophy) but doesn't use craft/lineage as a hard inclusion filter, and still skews toward already-famous names.

The result: a genuinely exceptional single-focus shokunin restaurant with no marketing budget and no Instagram appeal is invisible across all 10 sources unless a visitor already knows exactly what they're looking for. **That's the gap this site fills** — a directory where the *only* qualifying criterion is the craft test from `niche.md`, not popularity, price, or virality.
